## Claim

International public adaptation finance from developed to developing countries
**fell to $26bn in 2023**, from $28bn in 2022, against a modelled need of
**$310–365bn per year by 2035**.

## Quoted passage

> "adaptation finance needs of developing countries range from US$310 billion to
> US$365 billion per year by 2035. Meanwhile, international public adaptation
> finance flows from developed to developing countries fell from US$28 billion in
> 2022 to US$26 billion in 2023."

## Arithmetic worth doing in public

- **$26bn ÷ $310bn = 8.4 %** of the modelled need.
- The **Glasgow goal** was to double adaptation finance to ~$40bn by 2025. The
  flow went **down**, not up.
- The shortfall is roughly **$284bn/yr** — about **11×** what is being delivered.
- $26bn is about **0.6 %** of the ~$4.5tn/yr the world spends on fossil fuel
  subsidies and investment, depending on whose definition you use. The comparison
  is crude; the order of magnitude is not.

## The distinction this claim refuses to blur

- This is **public** adaptation finance from **developed to developing** countries.
  It excludes private finance, domestic spending and mitigation finance. All of
  those exist and some are larger.
- "Adaptation finance" is not "loss and damage". The Loss and Damage Fund has
  pledges of ~$790m, of which ~$397m was paid in — about **1.5 %** of one year of
  the adaptation gap. These are separate instruments and are routinely conflated.
- Needs estimates are modelled, and the report says so: $310bn is "based on
  modelled costs", while extrapolated stated needs give a higher range. The number
  is a floor chosen by method, not a discovery.

## Why the direction matters more than the level

A flow that falls from $28bn to $26bn while the need rises is not a stalled
programme. It is a programme moving away from its target, which is why the
report's own language is that the Glasgow and NCQG goals "will not be achieved"
unless trends turn around.
