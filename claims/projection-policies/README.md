## Claim

If policies in place continue, there is a 66 % chance that warming is held to
**2.8 °C** by 2100 — and it keeps rising after that.

## Quoted passage

> "If current policies continue, there is a 66 per cent chance that warming will
> be limited to 2.8°C by 2100, while continuing to increase thereafter (figure
> ES.5). If all NDCs are delivered and similar efforts continue, there is a 66
> per cent chance that warming will be limited to 2.6°C by 2100."

## Three things the sentence hides

1. **"limited to" is doing a lot of work.** The same report gives the range as
   **2.2–3.8 °C** and, elsewhere, a **10 % chance of exceeding 3.4 °C**. 2.8 is a
   66 %-confidence central estimate, not a ceiling.
2. **It moved the wrong way.** The previous edition's central estimate was lower;
   this one is 0.2–0.3 °C higher, and the report says so: the formal US
   withdrawal from Paris, plus revised historical emissions. New NDCs since last
   year had a "near-zero impact".
3. **The 66 % is a probability statement about a model ensemble**, not a forecast
   with a date. "By 2100" also means warming is still climbing at 2100 in this
   scenario — 2.8 °C is a waypoint, not a plateau.

## What it does not say

- It does not say 2.8 °C is inevitable. It is what current policy implies; policy
  is the variable.
- It does not say the Paris Agreement failed. It says the instruments in place are
  insufficient, which is a different claim with a different remedy.
- It does not say 2.8 °C is civilisation-ending. It says it is far above the
  agreed goal and above what adaptation is currently funded for.

## The counter-number worth keeping

The same report notes the trajectory has fallen from "around 4 °C before Paris to
2.8 °C today". That is the strongest evidence that the process has done
something, even while failing at the target.
